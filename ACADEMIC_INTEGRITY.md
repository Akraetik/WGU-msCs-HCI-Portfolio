# Academic integrity — what's in this repo and what isn't

I want this repo to help other students without helping anyone cheat. WGU's Student Code of Conduct prohibits sharing assessment answers or creating content that helps others get around the work. WGU's licensed learning materials are for enrolled students only.

## In this repo

- My own descriptions of what I did, in my own words, written after the fact
- Public course descriptions, paraphrased
- Skills, concepts, and vocabulary that any textbook covers
- Book titles, authors, and publishers
- Outside courses I took, with public links
- What I'd do differently: my own reflection
- **Rebuilds:** original projects of my own design that use the same skills on different problems and public data

## Not in this repo

- My submitted papers, code, wireframes, or videos
- WGU task prompts, scenarios, rubrics, starter code, or provided datasets
- Rubric section letters or a section-by-section walk-through of any task
- Links into WGU's GitLab, course pages, or e-book reader
- My student ID

## Before you post your own

1. **Read the policy.** Read WGU's Student Code of Conduct before you post. If you're unsure about something, ask your instructor.
2. **When in doubt, rebuild.** Use the same skill on a new problem with public data. It's better for your portfolio anyway, because you can explain every choice.
3. **Strip identifiers.** Remove your student ID, WGU usernames, and repo URLs from anything you post.
4. **Respect brands.** If you critique a real product, use your own wireframes and screenshots under fair-use limits, not copied logos and brand art.

*If anyone at WGU sees something here that crosses a line, open an issue or contact me and I'll take it down.*
